# Scott Howard Tennis — Web Site Report

## Web site goals

Scott described his goals this way:

> “I guess I would want them to think I was the best instructor around, and for the website to look professional and to find a way for it to be highly visible.”

He described the desired visitor action as:

> “To sprint to their phone and call me or immediately send an email. I don’t have it set up like Paul does to take lesson reservation signups.”

These goals are connected:

1. **Perceived excellence:** Show why Scott is an unusually qualified coach.
2. **Professional presentation:** Build trust through polished design, clear information, and a secure experience.
3. **Visibility:** Help Bay Area players and parents find Scott through local search.

The intended visitor path is simple: **find the site → trust Scott → contact Scott.**

Text, WhatsApp, and the short inquiry form extend this direct-contact goal; they do not require a booking system.

## Current information hierarchy

The site currently has five main pages:

- About
- Lessons Info
- Coaching Experience
- Testimonials
- Contact

The current information hierarchy is sound. It serves the goal of establishing Scott's credibility as a coach.

The main opportunity is to make **important decision information easier to find**. Visitors should not have to piece together **who each service is for**, **where lessons happen**, **what happens next**, and **how to contact Scott**.

## Suggested information hierarchy

The revised structure should follow the visitor’s decision path. It does not require a large site.

1. [**Home**](#home): Positioning, proof, key services, testimonials, and direct contact.
2. [**Why Scott**](#why-scott): Coaching philosophy, credentials, and teaching approach.
3. [**Coaching Programs**](#coaching-programs): Adult, junior, doubles, group, league, and match coaching.
4. [**Rates & Policies**](#rates--policies): Rates, Venmo, locations, cancellation, weather, and expectations.
5. [**Contact**](#contact): Phone, text, WhatsApp, email, response time, and an optional inquiry form.

6. [**Shared Footer on all pages**](#shared-footer): Persistent contact links, an inquiry-form link, and links to Scott’s Google and Yelp profiles.

## Visibility and reputation

Create and verify one Google Business Profile. Use accurate service areas, phone, website, photos, services, and hours or availability guidance. If clients do not visit Scott’s own address, use a service-area setup rather than implying a public storefront. Google provides Business Profiles for eligible in-person service businesses at no charge. [Google Business Profile guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7039811)

The profile is an external discovery and reputation channel, not a page in the site hierarchy. Keep the site’s primary actions direct: call, text, WhatsApp, email, or inquiry form.

Claim a free Yelp page as a secondary listing. Keep the name, phone, website, and service area consistent. Do not buy Yelp advertising until the free listing produces measurable inquiries.

Ask satisfied clients for honest reviews. Do not offer incentives. Monitor new reviews and reply professionally as a recurring activity.

## Platform and cost options

Because this is a small brochure site, it may not need WordPress hosting.

- **Carrd Pro Standard:** $19/year. Easiest low-cost option for a compact site with forms, analytics, custom domains, and meta tags. [Carrd](https://carrd.com/pro)
- **Cloudflare Pages:** Free static hosting. Best when a technical person will maintain the site. [Cloudflare Pages](https://www.cloudflare.com/products/pages/)
- **GitHub Pages:** Free hosting with custom-domain support. Best when a technical person will maintain the site. [GitHub Pages](https://docs.github.com/en/pages/configuring-a-custom-domain-for-your-github-pages-site/about-custom-domains-and-github-pages)

Compare total annual cost, including domain, email, forms, maintenance, and future edits.
